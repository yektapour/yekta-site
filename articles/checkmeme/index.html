CHECKMEME | The Move: A Painting That Talks Back

At first glance, it is a simple game of chess. A man in a suit on one side, a figure in a hoodie on the other, and a board full of pieces between them. Look a little longer and the table stops being a table. It is a battlefield of ideologies. CHECKMEME: The Move is my submission to The Memes by 6529, and it is not only a painting. It is an interactive meme: a painting you can play.

The idea is older than the internet. In 1837 a painting circulated under the name "Satan Playing Chess with Man, for His Soul." Two players, one board, and everything at stake. That image stayed with me. The game it describes never ended; only the players changed.

Same game. New players. In CHECKMEME the board is still there, the stakes are still there, but the pieces have changed sides of history.

On one side sits the old system: a formal suit, an expensive gold watch, an emblem of financial power and entrenched government. His pieces are uniform, black and identical, like riot police or sanitized politicians stripped of individuality. On the other side sits a figure in a hoodie: a symbol of the new generation, of programmers and digital native activists. His pieces are not conventional figures but memes and internet born characters: Pepe, Doge, Wojak, Grumpy Cat, Arthur's Fist. Power is no longer held only by those in suits.

This piece is personal. As someone who has felt the heavy shadow of war and dictatorship, it reflects my deepest wish: a transition from physical warfare to strategic battles. History has shown us that focusing power on a single individual often turns people into cogs in a killing machine. I believe true power no longer lies in destruction, but in thought and expression.

Black made the move. It's White's turn. If you look closely, the man in the suit has just played and is pulling his hand back. The traditional powers have already made their moves and played their games with the people. The painting captures that exact moment of transition. It says: enough is enough. The board is left to us. It is our turn to move.

Every piece is a meme. Each one keeps the core identity of the original, the frog, the dog, the fist, the cat, while adapting to a new role on the board. The diversity of characters and their emotions makes the piece a source for new memes, not just a picture of old ones.

That is why the piece is interactive. Tap anything and it reacts. Every piece talks back. Black always opens; White always gets the last word. The posters on the wall respond, the sign on the wall moves, the light follows your own time of day, and the gold watch keeps your time. A meme was never meant to sit still.

Every piece was drawn by hand before it was painted. From pencil sketch to finished figure, each character was designed to read clearly at a glance and still reward a closer look.

And you can look closer. The whole painting lives inside the piece at full resolution. Zoom in on the King of Kekistan, the riot visors, the brushstrokes on the wall. Every detail is there.

Some things are hidden. Some only appear at the right moment. I won't tell you what they are or where to look. The anon in the hoodie has not said a word yet. Maybe he is waiting for you.

The ideas in this painting don't belong only on a screen. Freedom to transact has already left the canvas and gone onto the streets.

CHECKMEME: The Move is a single self contained file, built to last on chain, and it works on desktop and mobile. Open it, turn the sound on, and tap the board. Then find the secrets. Your move. yektapour.com/interactive/checkmeme

    About

    YEKTA

    YEKTA is the name Hossein Yektapour paints under. A digital painter, he spent fifteen years as an art director making images for others before turning to his own.

    The work is conceptual and quiet: bodies that come apart, faces that turn into objects, and a red thread that keeps returning. It is the line that ties a thought to what it costs.

    overthink(paint)ing is how he names the process: thinking a feeling all the way through, until it has no choice but to become a picture.

    Alongside painting he makes interactive and generative work.
