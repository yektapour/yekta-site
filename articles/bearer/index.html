Bearer: Holding On to What Binds You

A bearer is someone who carries something. A message, a load, a responsibility. Sometimes a wound. In this painting, a heavy man hangs in empty space, folded into himself. A single red thread runs down from above, wraps around his body, and falls away below him. He is not tied to anything. There is no wall, no floor, no hook. And still he cannot let go.

1. A body with nowhere to stand

There is no room in this painting. No floor, no horizon, no place to rest. Only a stained, worn surface and a body floating in front of it. The man is not falling and not rising. He is held, somewhere in between, by a thread so thin it should not be able to hold anything at all. That is the first thing the painting asks: what keeps a person suspended, when nothing around them is solid?

2. Biting down

His eyes are shut hard. His face is pushed into his own arm, and he is biting down on it. It is the gesture of someone who has decided not to make a sound. Pain swallowed instead of spoken. The scream is there. It just has nowhere to go but back into the body.

3. It comes from above

The thread enters from the top edge of the painting. We never see where it begins. That is deliberate. Some of what we carry was handed down to us before we could choose: by family, by place, by history, by the people who came before. It arrives from outside the frame, and it lands on the shoulders.

4. Holding on to what binds him

Look at his hand. The thread is not only wrapped around him. He is gripping it. This is the center of the painting for me. The thing that binds him is also the thing he holds on to. If he let go, he might fall. If he holds on, it keeps tightening. Most of us know that knot. A grief, a duty, a story about ourselves. It hurts, and it is also what keeps us in one piece.

5. Flesh as weight

The body is painted heavy on purpose. Thick, uneven strokes. Skin in bruised pinks, greys and greens, the colors of flesh that has been carrying something for a long time. Weight is the subject here, not beauty. You should feel the mass of him, and wonder how a thread that thin can hold it.

6. Wrapped, turn by turn

Below the waist the thread keeps going. Around the thigh. Around the knee. Around both ankles. It doesn't happen at once. It wraps the way habits do, the way years do: one more turn, then another, until the legs can no longer move apart.

7. It goes on without him

At the bottom, the thread leaves his foot and keeps going, out of the frame. It came before him, and it will continue after him. He is not its beginning or its end. He is the part in the middle that carries it for a while. That is what a bearer is.

8. The weight of voices

Bearer also exists as an interactive work. The painting sits on a sheet of paper. When you speak into the microphone, the paper crumples, and the louder your voice, the tighter it folds around him. When you fall silent, it slowly opens again. Every voice leaves a fold. Words, shouts, the things people say to us, all of it presses on the body. And in the quiet, it tries to open back up. You can try it here: yektapour.com/interactive/bearer

    About

    YEKTA

    YEKTA is the name Hossein Yektapour paints under. A digital painter, he spent fifteen years as an art director making images for others before turning to his own.

    The work is conceptual and quiet: bodies that come apart, faces that turn into objects, and a red thread that keeps returning. It is the line that ties a thought to what it costs.

    overthink(paint)ing is how he names the process: thinking a feeling all the way through, until it has no choice but to become a picture.

    Alongside painting he makes interactive and generative work.
